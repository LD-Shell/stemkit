Thermostat and barostat

Barostat and thermostat guide

Thermostats

V-rescale recommended
Stochastic velocity rescaling (Bussi, Donadio and Parrinello 2007). It relaxes the temperature like a first-order coupling, so it is safe from the first step of NVT, and its random term gives the correct canonical distribution, so it is just as good in production. tau-t 0.1 ps (1 ps for Martini).
Nose-Hoover
An extended system with the correct ensemble and no random numbers. The temperature oscillates around the target, strongly if it starts far from it, so use it after equilibration. Its tau-t is the period of those oscillations: 4 to 5 times a first-order constant, 0.5 ps here (4 ps for Martini).
Berendsen deprecated
Suppresses the natural fluctuations of the kinetic energy, so the ensemble is wrong. Kept in GROMACS to reproduce old runs; grompp warns, so the job script adds -maxwarn.
tc-grps
Couple separately the parts that exchange heat slowly (solute and solvent, a bilayer and the water), but give every group hundreds of atoms at least, or its temperature is noise. Never a group per ion or per small ligand. Every atom must be in exactly one group.

Barostats

C-rescale recommended
Stochastic cell rescaling (Bernetti and Bussi 2020). It relaxes the pressure exponentially, so it is stable far from equilibrium, and its random term gives the correct volume fluctuations: the manual says it can be used for both equilibration and production. tau-p 1 ps (4 ps for Martini). In GROMACS 2025 it scales isotropically, semi-isotropically or with a surface tension, not anisotropically.
Parrinello-Rahman
The box vectors get an equation of motion. Correct fluctuations and any box shape, but it oscillates, very strongly if started away from the target pressure, and grompp notes that it is unstable with position restraints. So when you choose it, NPT equilibration here uses C-rescale and Parrinello-Rahman takes over in production, except with anisotropic scaling, which C-rescale cannot do. Its tau-p is a period: 5 ps (12 ps for Martini).
Berendsen deprecated
Wrong volume fluctuations; the manual recommends against it for new simulations and grompp warns.
tau-p and compressibility
tau-p is how quickly the box answers a pressure difference; too short and the box rings. The compressibility says how much the volume changes per bar (water at 300 K: 4.5e-5 bar−1). Together they set how hard the barostat pushes; the equilibrium density does not depend on them, so an approximate compressibility is fine.

Box scaling

Isotropic
x, y and z scale together and the box keeps its shape: liquids, solutions, proteins in water. One value each for ref-p and compressibility.
Semi-isotropic
The membrane plane (x/y) and the normal (z) scale separately, so the area per lipid and the thickness relax independently. Use it for bilayers and flat interfaces. Two values each: x/y, then z.
Anisotropic
All six box components scale on their own: crystals and solids under stress. The manual warns it can deform the box drastically. C-rescale cannot do it in GROMACS 2025, so the page uses Parrinello-Rahman instead (Berendsen can too, but is deprecated). Six values each; the off-diagonal compressibilities are 0, so the box angles stay fixed and the box must be rectangular: a rhombic dodecahedron or other triclinic box becomes too skewed as it shrinks, and mdrun stops.

Position restraints under pressure

A restrained atom is pulled towards a reference position. When the box scales, that reference has to move with it, or the restraints fight the barostat: grompp warns about absolute restraints with pressure coupling. refcoord-scaling = com scales the centre of mass of the references with the box and keeps their shape, so every restrained stage with a barostat gets it. The coordinates grompp reads with -r (here the start of that stage) are the references.

From the GROMACS 2025.1 manual: temperature coupling, pressure coupling and the reference manual on coupling algorithms.

Time step and bonds

Hydrogen mass repartitioning, 4 fs

Makes hydrogens three times heavier, taking the mass from the atom each is bonded to, so a 4 fs step stays stable. Needs GROMACS 2024 or newer.

What it does, and its limits

The fastest motions left once the bonds to hydrogen are rigid are the angles that move a hydrogen; they set the 2 fs limit. mass-repartition-factor = 3 makes grompp find the lightest atom (hydrogen, 1.008 u), raise every atom lighter than three times that to 3.024 u, and take the difference from the one atom it is bonded to. The total mass is unchanged, so densities and equilibrium properties are too, while those vibrations slow down enough for 4 fs.

  • 4 fs is the limit. The manual: with constraints = h-bonds, a factor of 3 "will usually enable a time step of 4 fs". Go beyond it and other motions become unstable; the page warns above 4 fs.
  • GROMACS 2024 or newer. The option arrived in 2024 (release notes). An older grompp does not know it and warns, so without -maxwarn it stops (the script passes none for this); with -maxwarn it would run without repartitioning, which at 4 fs crashes. For 2023 and older, repartition the topology itself (for example with ParmEd's HMassRepartition) and drop the option.
  • Dynamics run a little slower. The manual: it "does not affect equilibrium distributions, but makes dynamics slightly slower", so rates and diffusion from an HMR run need care.
  • grompp stops when a light atom has more than one bond, when the atom it is bonded to would become too light, or with perturbed masses in a free-energy run; it warns about light atoms with no bond, which cannot be repartitioned. It also notes that bonds such as C=O now oscillate in fewer than ten time steps, when the molecules have them (water and ions do not): expected at 4 fs with only the bonds to hydrogen rigid.
  • Water hydrogens are repartitioned too, which is harmless: rigid water has no internal motion.

      How-to guide

      How to build a GROMACS workflow or a batch script

      Pick an engine and work through its set-up; the files are written as you type. For GROMACS that is the whole run: a .mdp file for every stage (minimisation, NVT and NPT equilibration, production, and optionally annealing and pulling), each checked the way grompp checks it and explained line by line, the index groups your .mdp files need, and the job script that chains grompp and mdrun, all in one zip. For LAMMPS it writes the job script with the flags of the accelerator package you choose; for PLUMED it composes a plumed.dat from collective variables picked by category. It remembers your settings between visits.

      1

      System: the physics every stage shares

      Choose the force field (AMBER, CHARMM36, GROMOS 54A7, OPLS-AA or Martini 3), which sets the cut-offs, dispersion correction and time step it was parametrised with, and what you simulate: a solute in water, a membrane (semi-isotropic pressure coupling) or a liquid. Then the temperature and pressure, the thermostat and barostat (the guide next to them explains each), the rigid bonds and the time step, with hydrogen mass repartitioning for 4 fs.

      2

      Stages: what runs, and for how long

      Switch stages on or off and give each a length in ps or ns, its output intervals, and whether the solute is held by position restraints. Velocities are handled for you: new ones for the first dynamics stage, carried over through the checkpoint after it. The panel at the top shows the workflow at a glance, with the steps, frames and file sizes.

      3

      Index groups, when you need them

      Drop the .gro or .pdb grompp will read: you get the groups gmx make_ndx makes, suggestions for the ligand, the complex, the pocket, the membrane and the solvent, the temperature groups this system should use (applied to the .mdp files), and a builder for groups of your own. index.ndx goes into the zip and the script passes it with -n.

      4

      Job, then download all

      Set the scheduler (SLURM, PBS Pro / OpenPBS, LSF or Grid Engine), resources, GPU offload and an optional PLUMED file. Every file is on the right with a tab of its own, and Download all gives submit.sh, the .mdp files, index.ndx and a README in one zip. Put your coordinates and topology next to them and submit with the command shown: sbatch, qsub, or bsub < submit.sh for LSF.

      Two more tools sit beside the files. All options lists every .mdp option of GROMACS 2025.1 by section, with its default, values and the manual's text; set a value there and it goes into the file you choose. Check a file reads an .mdp you already have, says whether grompp will stop, explains every line, and opens it in the builder to continue from it.
      The generated script uses #!/bin/bash -e, requests memory explicitly, and writes per-job or per-array logs under logs/. These are deliberate HPC best practices, not noise, see the reasons in the next section. Anything the vendor leaves to the site (a GPU resource name, a parallel-environment name, a memory unit) is marked as site-specific in a comment inside the script.

      What the generator does, and why

      Every .mdp file checked like grompp

      The .mdp files are written from the GROMACS 2025.1 option table, generated from grompp's own reader and the manual, and each one is checked against grompp's consistency rules as you type. The page says, before you submit, whether grompp would stop, which warnings to expect and why, and adds -maxwarn only where a warning cannot be avoided.

      Index groups numbered as GROMACS numbers them

      The default groups follow gmx make_ndx exactly, quirks included, and the structure is read with GROMACS's own column rules, so a group name offered here is one grompp will find, holding the atoms it expects. One change is deliberate: the group make_ndx leaves nameless, for a blank residue name, is called Group, because GROMACS cannot read its own [  ] back. The temperature groups are checked to cover every atom exactly once, as grompp requires.

      Coupled force-field parameters

      The combination rule and 1‑4 fudge factors are not free choices: each force field ships a specific [ defaults ] line. Selecting a force field sets both for you, and any manual override that diverges from the canonical values is flagged.

      One request, four schedulers

      Explicit memory, wall-time validation, #!/bin/bash -e and per-job or per-array logs under logs/ follow published cluster best-practice guides so jobs fail loudly instead of silently. The same request becomes #SBATCH, #PBS, #BSUB or #$ directives, with the matching job-id, array-index and launch conventions.

      Hybrid CPU/GPU threading

      Under SLURM OMP_NUM_THREADS is tied to SLURM_CPUS_PER_TASK, and SRUN_CPUS_PER_TASK is exported for Slurm > 22.05; PBS sets NCPUS from ompthreads, which the script reads with the requested count as the fallback; LSF and Grid Engine expose only slot counts, so for them the requested thread count is written next to the directive it matches. gmx mdrun runs with -ntomp and -pin on for reproducible thread placement.

      Everything stays local

      The whole tool runs in your browser. Nothing you type is uploaded, logged, or sent anywhere, safe for unpublished systems and internal project names.

      The generated files are a correct starting point, not a validated production input. The checks here cover what the .mdp file alone decides; your topology decides the rest, so read grompp's own output (the page lists what to expect), and confirm module names, partitions and GPU flags against your own cluster's documentation.

      How the .mdp files are written

      Each .mdp file is complete and commented: every line says what it does, and the Explained view and the pointer over a line give the manual's meaning with a link to the option in the GROMACS 2025.1 manual.       • Stages. Minimisation uses steepest descent with flexible bonds and stops below the force you set; conjugate gradient, when chosen, runs after a steepest-descent stage of its own, which relieves the clashes of a freshly solvated system faster, and with flexible water (define = -DFLEXIBLE), because GROMACS cannot use SETTLE in conjugate gradient. NVT and NPT equilibration hold the solute with the topology's position restraints (define = -DPOSRES, and grompp -r with the stage's start coordinates); production releases them. Lengths in ps or ns become nsteps at the time step, output intervals become nstxout-compressed, nstenergy and nstlog, and nstcalcenergy is the largest divisor of nstenergy up to 100.
      • Velocities. The first dynamics stage draws new velocities (gen-vel = yes, continuation = no); every later one carries them over (gen-vel = no, continuation = yes), and grompp reads the previous stage's checkpoint with -t. Each stage can override this.
      • Thermostat and barostat. V-rescale and C-rescale are the defaults, as the GROMACS manual recommends. When Parrinello-Rahman is chosen, NPT equilibration uses C-rescale, because Parrinello-Rahman oscillates when the box starts away from equilibrium; when the box must scale anisotropically, which C-rescale does not support in GROMACS 2025, Parrinello-Rahman is used instead of C-rescale, and the box must be rectangular. A Berendsen barostat, when chosen, is kept in every stage, with -maxwarn. Restrained stages under pressure get refcoord-scaling = com. A membrane couples semi-isotropically.
      • Hydrogen mass repartitioning adds mass-repartition-factor = 3 and a 4 fs time step to every dynamics stage. grompp makes the lightest atoms three times heavier, taking the mass from the atom each is bonded to; the option exists since GROMACS 2024.
      • What grompp will say. Each file is checked against grompp's consistency rules; the tab of every file shows the verdict. The job script passes -maxwarn only to a grompp call whose file draws warnings that cannot be avoided, with a comment naming them: every GROMOS topology draws one (its twin-range parametrisation), and a Berendsen thermostat or barostat, when chosen, draws one each.
      • Restarts. With Skip stages that already finished on (the default), each stage runs only while its final .gro is missing, and mdrun -cpi continues from its checkpoint, so a job that reaches its wall time can be submitted again and carries on; off, every stage runs from its start each time and no checkpoint is read. With a job array the .mdp files and an index built on the page stay in the submission directory and every task reads them there, so every run directory must hold the system that index was built from, atom for atom.
      • Index groups. The default groups port analyse() from GROMACS's index.cpp and match gmx make_ndx byte for byte, except that a nameless group is written as [ Group ]. make_ndx writes [  ] for a blank residue name, which GROMACS cannot read back: it takes the brackets for atom numbers and adds the group's atoms to the group before it. The suggested temperature groups couple solute and solvent (or membrane and solvent) separately when each is large enough, and are checked to cover every atom once, as grompp's do_numbering requires.

      Zips downloaded from this page were run with GROMACS 2025 (grompp and a short mdrun for every stage) on an AMBER protein with C-rescale, a CHARMM bilayer with semi-isotropic coupling, a GROMOS system as a job array with -maxwarn, a 4 fs run with hydrogen mass repartitioning, simulated annealing with OPLS-AA, steered pulling between groups built on the page with Parrinello-Rahman in production, and a coarse-grained system with Martini settings.

      The parameters behind the topology header

      A GROMACS [ defaults ] directive declares the non-bonded function, the combination rule, whether 1‑4 pairs are generated, and the 1‑4 scaling factors fudgeLJ and fudgeQQ. These are properties of the force field and must match it.

      The [ defaults ] line has the form:

      [ defaults ]
      ; nbfunc  comb-rule  gen-pairs  fudgeLJ  fudgeQQ

      nbfunc selects the non-bonded form (1 = Lennard-Jones). comb-rule selects how pair parameters are combined: rule 2 is Lorentz–Berthelot (arithmetic–geometric), while rules 1 and 3 are geometric. gen-pairs = yes generates missing 1‑4 pairs from the normal LJ parameters scaled by fudgeLJ. fudgeLJ scales 1‑4 Lennard-Jones interactions (used only when gen-pairs is yes); fudgeQQ scales 1‑4 electrostatics and is always applied.

      Canonical values shipped by each force field:

      Force fieldcomb-rulegen-pairsfudgeLJfudgeQQ
      AMBER (e.g. 99SB-ILDN)2yes0.50.8333
      CHARMM36 / CHARMM36m2yes1.01.0
      OPLS-AA3yes0.50.5
      GROMOS 54A71no1.01.0
      Martini 32no1.01.0

      Because grompp reads whichever [ defaults ] line the topology supplies rather than inferring it from the force field name, an inconsistent combination (for example OPLS atom types with rule 2, or AMBER scaling on a CHARMM field) is accepted silently and yields physically wrong non-bonded and 1‑4 interactions. grompp also accepts only one [ defaults ] directive, and each force field's forcefield.itp already contains it, so the header includes that file and shows its values as a comment rather than writing a second copy. An override is written as instructions for editing a local copy of the force field, with a warning that it departs from the canonical values. AMBER99SB-ILDN, GROMOS 54A7 and OPLS-AA ship with GROMACS (amber99sb-ildn.ff, gromos54a7.ff, oplsaa.ff); CHARMM36 is a separate download from the MacKerell lab, and Martini 3 from cgmartini.nl, whose martini_v3.0.0.itp carries the [ defaults ] line. GROMOS lists its 1‑4 pairs explicitly, so it generates none. The topology header is not in the zip, so it cannot overwrite the topology you already have.

      GPU offload & mdrun performance

      The GPU flags follow the GROMACS developers' own performance guidance. mdrun can offload the short-range non-bonded (-nb), long-range PME (-pme), bonded (-bonded) and update/constraints (-update) work to the GPU. The tool enforces the documented rules:

      • PME on GPU allows only one PME rank. When more than one rank is requested with -pme gpu, the tool adds -npme 1 automatically.
      • PME and bonded forces run on the GPU only in dynamics. mdrun refuses -pme gpu and -bonded gpu for energy minimisation, and -pme gpu for a file without PME electrostatics (Martini’s reaction field), so the script leaves them out of those stages, with a comment above the mdrun line.
      • Thread-MPI or MPI. The thread-MPI gmx gets -ntmpi (1 unless you set it): with a GPU in use, a thread-MPI mdrun given -ntomp stops without it. The MPI build gmx_mpi rejects -ntmpi; it takes its ranks from srun or mpirun, which the script uses when you ask for more than one node. Double-precision builds have no GPU support, so they get no GPU flags.
      • -bonded gpu requires -nb gpu. Bonded offload is part of the same particle-particle workload, so the short-range non-bonded task must also be on the GPU; the tool warns if it is not.
      • GPU-resident mode (-update gpu) cannot constrain all bonds of a protein (choose bonds to hydrogen), and mdrun refuses it with Nose-Hoover, virtual sites (a TIP4P water) and in minimisation, so the script leaves it out of every stage that has one of those and says why. It disables dynamic load balancing, and benefits from infrequent temperature/pressure coupling and a larger nstcalcenergy (the developers suggest coupling every ≥ 50–100 steps).
      • Use constraints = h-bonds, not all-bonds, with a 2 fs step: it is faster, required for GPU-resident mode, and is what most force fields were parametrised with. GROMOS is the exception: it was parametrised with every bond rigid, so the page writes all-bonds for it, and mdrun then refuses -update gpu for a protein.
      • 1–3 MPI ranks per GPU is generally optimal; with GPU-resident mode and direct GPU communication, 1 rank/GPU is usually best.
      • After a run, check ns/day and the PP/PME balance at the end of md.log to see where time is going.

      The developers' reference example for a single fast GPU with a weak CPU is gmx mdrun -ntmpi 1 -nb gpu -pme gpu -bonded gpu -update gpu. The toggles default to -nb gpu -pme gpu, with -ntmpi 1 for the thread-MPI gmx; energy minimisation keeps only -nb gpu, and a stage without PME drops -pme gpu. Tick -bonded gpu and -update gpu to get the full example.

      LAMMPS accelerator packages & benchmarking

      LAMMPS ships five accelerator packages, each appending a suffix to any supported style. The tool emits the correct command-line switches for each:

      PackageSuffixHardwareSwitches
      GPUgpuNVIDIA / AMD / Intel GPUs-sf gpu -pk gpu N
      KOKKOSkkGPUs, Xeon Phi, OpenMP-k on g N -sf kk -pk kokkos
      INTELintelIntel CPUs / Xeon Phi-sf intel -pk intel 0
      OPENMPompMulticore CPUs (hybrid)-sf omp -pk omp N
      OPToptGeneric CPU pair styles-sf opt

      LAMMPS parallelises with MPI, so the SLURM request uses --ntasks-per-node (ranks), not --cpus-per-task. The OPENMP package is the exception: it is a hybrid MPI × OpenMP scheme, so it uses both, srun -n R -c T lmp -sf omp -pk omp T, where -pk omp T matches --cpus-per-task. For GPU and KOKKOS runs, the number of MPI ranks per node usually matches the number of GPUs per node.

      Benchmarking is essential. Acceleration is not automatically faster: the fastest choice depends on your system size, the specific pair/bond styles, and your hardware. Offloading a calculation to the GPU can leave the CPU idle, and adding cores past the optimum can actually slow a run down. Start small, read the Performance and per-section timing lines LAMMPS prints at the end of the run, and only scale up the combination that measurably helps. Not every style has an accelerated variant, check the style's doc page for the available suffixes.

      PLUMED collective variables & enhanced sampling

      The PLUMED tab builds a plumed.dat file for PLUMED 2.9, 2.10 or 2.11, chosen in the header of the System and setup panel, and checks its keywords, defaults and modules against that release. An action the chosen release lacks stays in the CV list but is marked (“needs PLUMED ≥ 2.10”, or “not in PLUMED 2.10”) and cannot be added; where the chosen release has a replacement, the page names it. Collective variables are grouped by category and each exposes the exact keywords from the PLUMED source, so you get correct syntax without reading the code. Add one or more CVs, give them labels, then choose a bias method.

      A CV line has the form label: ACTION KEY=VALUE ..., for example phi: TORSION ATOMS=5,7,9,15. Labels become the ARG list of the bias. The generator supports standard, well-tempered (adding BIASFACTOR and TEMP) and parallel-bias (PBMETAD) metadynamics, OPES (OPES_METAD), a harmonic RESTRAINT for umbrella windows, a MOVINGRESTRAINT for steered MD, UPPER_WALLS and LOWER_WALLS, and ABMD.

      Speed optimisations (from the PLUMED performance guidance):

      • Store the bias on a grid (GRID_MIN/MAX/BIN). Without it, every step re-sums all deposited hills, so a long metadynamics run slows down continuously; with a grid the cost stays flat.
      • CALC_RCT with RCT_USTRIDE>1 computes the time-dependent c(t) reweighting factor on the fly; a larger stride reduces its overhead. It requires the bias to be on a grid.
      • Set D_MAX on the switching function of every multicolvar (COORDINATIONNUMBER, the Steinhardt Q3/Q4/Q6 and their LOCAL_ forms, TETRAHEDRAL). These sum over every pair of atoms; with D_MAX set, PLUMED finds the pairs closer than it with linked cells instead of scanning them all, which the developers call a "colossal speedup". The tool folds R_0/D_0/D_MAX into a single SWITCH={RATIONAL ...} block for you and warns if D_MAX is missing.
      • A neighbour list for COORDINATION. COORDINATION is not a multicolvar and has no linked cells: it visits every pair on its list at every step, with or without D_MAX, which only brings the switching function to zero there. What speeds up a large COORDINATION is NLIST: PLUMED rebuilds the list of pairs closer than NL_CUTOFF every NL_STRIDE steps and computes only those in between, and for a small group against a large one (a ligand in water) it asks the MD code for the nearby atoms only. Set NL_CUTOFF well beyond where the switch has decayed, and NL_STRIDE short enough that no atom comes into range between two updates. The tool warns if NLIST is set without both, or with NL_CUTOFF under twice R_0.
      • WALKERS_MPI runs multiple walkers that share one bias, which fills the free-energy surface faster on an MPI build.
      Keyword availability depends on your PLUMED build and version: set the version to what plumed info --version prints on the machine that runs the job, and read an action's keywords there with plumed manual --action=<NAME>. The file's header names the target release and the command that checks the file before the run, plumed driver --natoms N --parse-only --plumed plumed.dat, with N the number of atoms. Grid bounds default to the CV range placeholders (e.g. -pi/pi for torsions), set them to your actual CV limits.

      Structure / order parameters

      The Steinhardt parameters Q3, Q4 and Q6 are bond-orientational order parameters that quantify local crystalline structure, the standard way to follow nucleation, melting and phase changes. They act on a SPECIES group with a switching function (R_0, D_0), and the MEAN flag reduces the per-atom values to one scalar CV suitable for biasing. Q6 is the most widely used; Q4 helps separate cubic (FCC/BCC) environments. A per-atom COORDINATIONNUMBER (local neighbour count) is offered in the same category.

      Custom CVs

      The Custom category lets you write any PLUMED action line directly, the tool only prepends the label. Use it for CVs outside the catalogue, for optional keywords a preset does not expose, or to combine existing CVs with the CUSTOM/MATHEVAL function action, e.g. diff: CUSTOM ARG=d1,d2 FUNC=x-y PERIODIC=NO. Whatever you type is passed through verbatim, so it is as flexible as writing plumed.dat by hand while keeping the labelling, bias wiring and PRINT list consistent.

      The scheduler side

      The batch script follows widely published cluster best-practice guides, whichever scheduler is selected.

      • #!/bin/bash -e, a failing command aborts the job immediately, so failures surface as FAILED in sacct (or the equivalent) instead of continuing with a corrupt state. Whether the -e on that first line reaches bash depends on how the scheduler starts the script: Grid Engine's default shell_start_mode ignores the shebang line and runs the script through the -S shell, and LSF's documentation does not say whether the options on it survive, so the PBS, LSF and Grid Engine scripts repeat it as an explicit set -e.
      • Explicit memory, without it, many sites apply a small default (often ~1 GB/CPU) that quietly kills MD jobs. The request is translated into each scheduler's own unit: --mem=32G, mem=32gb in a PBS chunk, rusage[mem=32768] in MB for LSF, h_vmem=32G for Grid Engine.
      • OMP_NUM_THREADS=$SLURM_CPUS_PER_TASK (plus SRUN_CPUS_PER_TASK for Slurm > 22.05), prevents thread over-subscription in hybrid runs. PBS sets NCPUS from ompthreads for rank 0, so the script reads it with the requested count as the fallback; LSF and Grid Engine expose only slot counts, so the requested value is written in, next to the directive it matches.
      • Only set --cpus-per-task for multithreaded codes and ntasks>1 for MPI codes, gmx mdrun is the hybrid case handled here.
      • Minimal partition specification, leaving the partition or queue unset (unless special hardware is needed) gives the scheduler more freedom and shortens queue time.
      • Request only what you use, over-requesting CPUs, memory or GPUs leaves resources idle (nobody else can use them) and lengthens your own queue time. Get a job working on one GPU and confirm it is actually used before scaling up, and size memory from the job's real peak usage (e.g. the MaxRSS field in sacct).

      Frequently asked questions

      Why did the tool add -npme 1 or warn about my GPU flags?
      These are GROMACS rules. PME on a GPU supports only one PME rank, so -npme 1 is added when you request -pme gpu with more than one rank. -bonded gpu requires -nb gpu because bonded offload is part of the same particle-particle workload. -pme gpu, -bonded gpu and -update gpu run only in dynamics, so energy minimisation keeps -nb gpu alone; -pme gpu needs PME electrostatics, so a Martini (reaction-field) stage leaves it out; and -update gpu (GPU-resident mode) cannot constrain all bonds of a protein and is refused with Nose-Hoover or virtual sites such as TIP4P’s, so stages with those leave it out. The script says above each mdrun line what it left out and why. The thread-MPI gmx also gets -ntmpi 1, which a GPU run given -ntomp needs, and the MPI build gmx_mpi never gets -ntmpi, which it rejects.
      How are the GROMACS stages chained together?
      Each stage's grompp reads the previous stage's .gro via -c, and, when velocities carry over, its checkpoint via -t (NVT → NPT → Production); the .mdp file then says continuation = yes and gen-vel = no. Energy minimisation writes no checkpoint, so the first dynamics stage draws new velocities instead. When a stage has position restraints, -r is added pointing at the same coordinate file. Production has restraints switched off by default, and a warning appears if you tick them. With Skip stages that already finished on (the default), each stage is skipped once its final .gro exists, so a resubmitted job carries on where it stopped; off, every stage runs from its start each time.
      Which thermostat and barostat should I use?
      V-rescale and C-rescale, unless you have a reason not to. Both relax like the old Berendsen methods, so they are stable from the first step, and both have a random term that gives the correct ensemble, so the GROMACS manual recommends C-rescale for equilibration and production alike. Parrinello-Rahman is a sound alternative in production (the page then equilibrates with C-rescale, because Parrinello-Rahman oscillates far from equilibrium), and of the barostats that are not deprecated, the only one that scales anisotropically, so the page uses it for anisotropic scaling in every stage, with a rectangular box. Berendsen gives wrong fluctuations and is kept only to reproduce old runs; grompp warns. The guide under the thermostat and barostat on the System view explains each, with tau-t, tau-p, the compressibility and semi-isotropic coupling for membranes.
      What does hydrogen mass repartitioning do, and which GROMACS do I need?
      With mass-repartition-factor = 3, grompp makes every hydrogen three times heavier and takes the extra mass from the atom it is bonded to, so the total mass, and with it the equilibrium properties, do not change, while the fast angle vibrations of hydrogens slow down enough for a 4 fs time step with the bonds to hydrogen constrained. The dynamics run slightly slower, which matters for rates and diffusion. The option exists since GROMACS 2024; for older versions repartition the topology itself. grompp notes that bonds such as C=O then oscillate in fewer than ten steps, which is expected (a box of water and ions has no such bonds, and no note).
      Why does submit.sh pass -maxwarn for GROMOS?
      grompp warns for every GROMOS topology, because the force field was parametrised with a twin-range cut-off GROMACS no longer has, and without -maxwarn a single warning stops it. The page counts the warnings each .mdp file will draw and passes exactly that many, with a comment above the call naming each one; files that draw none get no -maxwarn, so an unexpected warning still stops the job.
      Where do the temperature groups come from, and when do I need an index file?
      From what you simulate: Protein Non-Protein for a solute in water, which GROMACS makes by itself, System for a liquid, and SOLU MEMB SOLV for a membrane, as CHARMM-GUI names them in its index file. Load your structure under Index groups and the page recommends groups for it, builds index.ndx, checks that the groups cover every atom exactly once, and passes the file with -n. Any name GROMACS does not make itself needs an index file.
      Can I check, or continue from, an .mdp file I already have?
      Yes: paste or drop it under Check a file. It is read the way grompp reads it, and you get the verdict (will grompp stop, and at how many warnings), every problem with its line and a link to the manual, and the file explained line by line. Open in the builder then fills the form from it and keeps every option the builder would write differently as one you set, so the files you download say the same as yours.
      Can I set an option the builder has no field for?
      Yes, under All options: every .mdp option of GROMACS 2025.1, by section, with its default, unit, allowed values and the manual's text. Set a value for one file or for all of them; the file then shows it with a comment, and Reset to generated puts back what the builder writes.
      Why does LAMMPS use tasks instead of CPUs per task?
      LAMMPS parallelises with MPI, so you request --ntasks-per-node (MPI ranks per node), whereas GROMACS mdrun is threaded and uses --cpus-per-task. The one exception is the LAMMPS OPENMP package, a hybrid MPI × OpenMP scheme that uses both, the tool then sets -pk omp N to match CPUs per task. For GPU or KOKKOS runs, MPI ranks per node usually matches GPUs per node.
      Which LAMMPS accelerator should I pick?
      It depends on your hardware and your input's styles, and there is no universal answer, you must benchmark. GPU/KOKKOS need a GPU; INTEL and OPT speed up CPU-only runs; OPENMP is hybrid and helps on high-core-count nodes. Acceleration is not automatically faster: offloading can leave the CPU idle, and adding cores past the optimum can slow a run down. Read the Performance and timing breakdown LAMMPS prints at the end, and scale up only what measurably helps.
      How does the PLUMED builder help me?
      It groups the collective variables by category and, for each one, exposes the exact keywords defined in the PLUMED source, atom lists, switching-function parameters, flags like NOPBC or COMPONENTS, so you get correct plumed.dat syntax without reading the code. You add CVs, label them, pick a bias method (standard, well-tempered or parallel-bias MetaD, OPES, a harmonic or moving restraint, upper or lower walls, or ABMD), and toggle speed options. The output targets PLUMED 2.9, 2.10 or 2.11, whichever you pick in the header of the System and setup panel, and its keywords are checked against that release.
      What is the difference between standard and well-tempered metadynamics here?
      Standard MetaD deposits Gaussian hills of fixed height. Well-tempered MetaD scales the hill height down over time using a BIASFACTOR (and TEMP), so the bias converges more smoothly. The builder adds those two keywords when you select the well-tempered variant, and OPES is offered as a modern alternative.
      Why does the PLUMED tab warn about grids and CALC_RCT?
      Metadynamics without a grid re-sums every deposited hill each step, so a long run slows down continuously, storing the bias on a grid keeps the cost flat. And CALC_RCT (on-the-fly c(t) reweighting) only works when the bias is on a grid, so the tool warns if you enable reweighting without it.
      Why does choosing a force field change the combination rule and fudge factors?
      Because they are physically coupled. Each GROMACS force field ships a specific [ defaults ] line: AMBER uses rule 2 with 0.5/0.8333, CHARMM36 uses rule 2 with 1.0/1.0, OPLS-AA uses rule 3 with 0.5/0.5, and GROMOS 54A7 rule 1 with no generated pairs. Mixing them produces a silently wrong topology. The same choice sets the cut-offs and time step of the .mdp files.
      What exactly are fudgeLJ and fudgeQQ?
      They scale the 1‑4 interactions (between atoms separated by three bonds). fudgeLJ scales the 1‑4 Lennard-Jones term and is applied only when gen-pairs is yes; fudgeQQ scales the 1‑4 electrostatics and is always applied.
      Can I override the auto-set parameters?
      Yes: enable Advanced override to edit the combination rule and fudge factors directly. Whenever your values differ from the force field's canonical ones, a warning explains the mismatch so you can confirm it is intentional.
      Why did the script add things I did not ask for (bash -e, memory, log files)?
      They are HPC best practices. #!/bin/bash -e makes the job stop and report FAILED on the first error; explicit --mem stops a small site default from killing the job; and --output/--error capture logs per job or per array task under logs/.
      Which schedulers are supported, and what changes between them?
      SLURM, PBS Professional / OpenPBS, IBM Spectrum LSF and Grid Engine (SGE, UGE). Pick one in the cluster card and the same request is written as #SBATCH, #PBS, #BSUB or #$ directives, with the matching job-id and array-index variables, the launch command (srun or mpirun with the task count taken from the allocation) and the submit command under the script. The mapping table in the method section lists every directive. The SLURM output is unchanged from earlier versions of this tool.
      Why does LSF need bsub < submit.sh rather than bsub submit.sh?
      bsub reads the #BSUB lines only when the script arrives on standard input. Given as an argument, the file runs as a plain command and every directive is ignored, so the job would be submitted with the queue defaults.
      What does "site-specific" mean in the generated script?
      A value the vendor documentation leaves to each installation: the GPU resource name under PBS (ngpus on most sites) or Grid Engine (no standard at all, so the script carries a commented placeholder), the Grid Engine parallel-environment name (qconf -spl lists them), the memory resource and its unit, and whether an LSF slot is a core. Check each against your cluster's documentation before submitting; the comment sits next to the line it concerns.
      Why does the submit hint start with mkdir -p logs?
      SLURM and Grid Engine open the output file as the job starts; if logs/ does not exist yet, the job fails before the script's own mkdir can run. PBS and LSF spool the output and copy it back when the job ends, so for them the mkdir inside the script is in time.
      Where are my settings kept, and how do I share them?
      In your browser's local storage, written a moment after each change and restored when you return; nothing leaves your device. Export settings downloads the same data as a JSON file so a group can share one cluster configuration, Import settings reads it back (unknown keys are ignored), and Reset to defaults forgets the saved state.
      Is this a validated production input?
      No. It is a correct, best-practice starting point: the .mdp files pass grompp's rules as the page reproduces them, and zips from the page have been run with GROMACS 2025, but your topology and system decide the rest. Read grompp's own output (the page lists what to expect), check the temperature, pressure and density of the equilibration before production, and check module names, partitions, GPU flags and account settings against your own cluster's documentation.
      Is my data uploaded anywhere?
      No. Everything runs locally in your browser. Nothing you type (job names, project directories, includes) leaves your device.
